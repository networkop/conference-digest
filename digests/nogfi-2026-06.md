### RFC8950 for a V4LESS(-AS) World: Diving into the magic of IPv4-with-IPv6 Next-Hops

**Links:** [Abstract](https://nog.fi/event/59/contributions/517/) · [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWK9bjlpux0)

Daniel Wagner (DE-CIX), Tobias Fiebig (TU Wien)

- **Why**: The most directly relevant fabric/interconnect talk on the program. Carrying IPv4 routes with IPv6 next-hops dissolves several standing design pains in both IXP peering LANs and large ECMP fabrics — exactly the DC/WAN interconnect territory this PM works in.
- **What**: A working walkthrough of what IPv4-with-IPv6 next-hops (RFC 8950, which extends RFC 5549-style extended next-hop encoding to eBGP) enables: IXPs without IPv4 peering-LAN growth pain, seamless IPv4 routing at /32 granularity, IPv4-free transport links, a fully ARP-free infrastructure, and ECMP-enabled L3 underlays for 100G+ fabrics without LACP. Includes an open invitation to peer with V4LESS-AS (AS215250), their live RFC 8950 testbed at an internet exchange.
- **How**: IPv4 NLRI is advertised over BGP sessions using IPv6 next-hops, so the entire L3 core — peering LANs, transport, fabric underlay — can be IPv6-only while still serving IPv4 destinations; with no IPv4 at L2/L3, ARP disappears and routed ECMP replaces LACP-bundled L2 links.
- **Where applicable**: A standards-based technique, not vendor- or org-specific in principle. Constraints: every router on the path must support extended next-hop encoding, and the ARP-free/no-LACP benefits are easiest to realize in greenfield or single-administrator fabrics. The V4LESS-AS testbed (AS215250) is the concrete proof point.

### ASPA - RPKI BGP Path Verification

**Links:** [Abstract](https://nog.fi/event/59/contributions/502/) · [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pAun5vP4RbY)

Tim Bruijnzeels (RIPE NCC)

- **Why**: RPKI today only validates route *origin*; ASPA is the next mechanism, validating AS *paths* and rejecting route leaks. Anyone responsible for BGP security needs to know where this stands operationally.
- **What**: A walkthrough of the ASPA object type and its verification algorithm, how to sign provider authorizations in the RIPE NCC RPKI Dashboard, how validation behaves in practice, and real uptake and lessons learned so far. *(from abstract only)*
- **How**: AS holders publish an ASPA object authorizing their upstream providers; validators use it to check received paths for customer→provider consistency, detecting and rejecting "valleys" — leaked paths — in addition to origin validation.
- **Where applicable**: General BGP technique, not org-specific. Requires RPKI CA participation and validator support on receiving routers; protection only materializes as transit providers adopt it, which makes the uptake discussion the key operational caveat.

### The IRR Landscape and its Implications on Global Internet Traffic

**Links:** [Abstract](https://nog.fi/event/59/contributions/519/) · [Slides](https://nog.fi/event/59/contributions/519/attachments/294/624/2026-06-16_DW_irr_landscape.pptx) · [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UK058KhIZUU)

Daniel Wagner (DE-CIX)

- **Why**: Route-server prefix filters at IXPs are generated from IRR data; when AS-SETs are inconsistent or stale, traffic that should flow through the peering fabric doesn't. This is the data-quality counterpart to the ASPA talk, attacking the same trust gap from the legacy side.
- **What**: Latest results from Wagner's RIPE Labs article series on the IRR landscape, including previously unreleased insights on AS-SET heterogeneity, IRR lookup ordering, and the measured traffic impact at a very large IXP, ending in a community call to action. *(from abstract only)*
- **How**: Analysis of IRR database contents and route-server filter-generation behavior, correlated with observed traffic at a large IXP.
- **Where applicable**: The measurement is DE-CIX-scale (org-specific), but the mechanism — filters generated from heterogeneous IRR data — applies to every IXP participant and most transit relationships. Direction of findings transfers; absolute numbers do not.

### A deep-dive into NLNOGs tools

**Links:** [Abstract](https://nog.fi/event/59/contributions/516/) · [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZBZwsInVI3E)

Nick Bouwhuis (NLNOG)

- **Why**: Free, community-maintained, vendor-neutral tooling that operator teams can adopt immediately — the highest transferability-per-effort item on the program.
- **What**: A tour of NLNOG's operator tools — the RING (shared shell access across member networks) and IRRexplorer (IRR/RPKI cross-checking), among others — what each is useful for, and the maintenance challenges of running community infrastructure. *(from abstract only)*

## Themes

- **Path-wide routing security is arriving**: ASPA moves RPKI beyond origin validation while the IRR-landscape work shows the legacy filter-generation stack measurably constraining traffic — two talks attacking the same interdomain trust gap from opposite sides.
- **IPv6 as the universal substrate**: RFC 8950 next-hops turn IPv6 into the transport for IPv4 service — ARP-free cores, IPv4-free links, ECMP L3 fabrics without LACP. IPv4-over-IPv6 is shifting from lab trick to operating model.
- **Portable automation and community tooling over vendor lock-in**: NLNOG's shared tools, vendor-neutral Python against native APIs, and the NAF vocabulary effort all push the same direction — skills and community infrastructure that outlive any vendor choice.
- **Cryptographic lifecycle as routine operations**: the root KSK rollover checklist and the PQC crypto-agility talk both put key and primitive transitions on the ops calendar rather than the research agenda.
- **Physical resilience under stress**: a 44-country network kept online through literal fire and cable cuts anchors the program's physical-infrastructure thread; the local-root DNS debate echoes the same resilience instinct at the resolver layer.
- **AI on both ends of the wire**: selling GPU compute (and its virtualization pain) on the demand side, and MCP-driven local AI agents operating backup products on the ops side.
