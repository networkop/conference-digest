### MetaRoCE: Rethinking RDMA for the AI presented by Meta

**Links:** [Abstract](https://2026ocpglobal.fnvirtual.app/a/schedule/10079/)

Arvind Srinivasan (Meta), Sandeep Nagaraja (Meta), Balakrishnan Raman (AMD)

- **Why**: A clean-sheet replacement for RoCEv2 from one of the largest GPU-fleet operators, now being opened as a spec — directly in the PM's lossless-fabrics/RDMA core interest and a candidate competitor/complement to MRC (below).
- **What**: MetaRoCE, a multipath, out-of-order, receiver-driven RDMA transport that treats Ethernet as lossy by design rather than patching lossless fabrics with more tuning; Meta is publishing the specification for industry adoption (from abstract only).
- **How**: All transport intelligence is pushed into the NIC; multipath and receiver-driven delivery replace PFC-style losslessness as the reliability model.
- **Why it matters for triage**: Paired with MRC and SUE-T, this is the third open transport spec from a hyperscaler this cycle — the field's verdict that decade-old RoCEv2 is done at 10k+ GPU scale.
- **Where applicable**: Meta production (tens of thousands of GPUs); AMD co-presenting suggests NIC-side implementation requirements — adoption depends on NIC vendor support, so watch the ecosystem response.

### Enabling AI Networking @ Scale with Multi-path Reliable Connections (MRC)

**Links:** [Abstract](https://2026ocpglobal.fnvirtual.app/a/schedule/8964/) · [Materials](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-mrc-1-0-pdf)

Guohan Lu (OpenAI), Eric Spada (Broadcom Inc.)

- **Why**: MRC is now a ratified OCP specification co-authored by OpenAI and Broadcom — a concrete, open alternative for AI-fabric transport that a PM in this space will need to track against MetaRoCE and Falcon.
- **What**: MRC combines multipath transport, adaptive load balancing, reliable delivery, and path-aware congestion management to keep utilization high and tail latency low in fabrics of hundreds of thousands of XPUs (from abstract only).
- **How**: Multipath reliable connections at the transport layer with awareness of per-path congestion state; the OCP MRC 1.0 document is the spec itself, which the session walks through.
- **Where applicable**: Designed for open Ethernet-based AI infrastructure broadly, not one operator's fabric; requires switch/NIC support for the MRC semantics (Broadcom involvement signals merchant-silicon intent).

### ESUN: Open Ethernet for the Scale-Up Era

Pratik Marolia (Microsoft), Manoj Wadekar (Meta)

- **Why**: Scale-up (in-rack/in-pod XPU interconnect) was the last proprietary holdout — NVLink territory. ESUN 1.0, ratified by OCP in March 2026 with 170+ contributors from 20+ organizations including NVIDIA itself, is the strongest signal yet that open Ethernet is expanding from scale-out into scale-up.
- **What**: An operator-requirements specification for Ethernet-based scale-up networks: minimizing header/framing overhead designed for general internet traffic, and breaking single-vendor interconnect lock-in (from abstract only).
- **How**: Cross-industry coalition (Meta, Microsoft, AMD, Arista, Broadcom, Cisco, Marvell, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Oracle) producing an OCP spec in months; the talk covers the what and the roadmap for future versions.
- **Where applicable**: Industry-wide; it is a requirements/spec document, so its impact depends on silicon (Tomahawk-class and similar) implementing it — pair with the SUE-T and Tahan talks for the transport and hardware instantiations.

### Tahan: An Open, Ethernet-Based Scale-Up Switch for Meta's MTIA Accelerators

**Links:** [Abstract](https://2026ocpglobal.fnvirtual.app/a/schedule/9723/)

### Integrated Optics at Scale for AI Infrastructure: From 51.2T CPO Validation to Meta's 102.4T Integrated Optics Switch

### Clustermapper: Deterministic path diagnosis for AI backend networks using SRv6

**Links:** [Abstract](https://2026ocpglobal.fnvirtual.app/a/schedule/9467/) · [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04333) · [Materials](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-mrc-1-0-pdf)

Ryder Lewis (OpenAI), Guohan Lu (OpenAI)

- **Why**: Turns path diagnosis from statistical inference into ground truth — a genuinely clever use of SRv6 source routing, and the operational companion to the MRC spec from the same OpenAI team.
- **What**: A path-diagnosis system for MRC/SRv6 AI backend networks: a lightweight agent on every node sends SRv6 source-routed self-probes through T0/T1 switch paths, producing a deterministic link-health map used to localize faults (NIC-T0 links, T0-T1 links, switch forwarding) and turn urgent link-failure incidents into scheduled maintenance (from abstract only; the linked arXiv paper — titled "Resilient AI Supercomputer Networking using MRC and SRv6", 18 pages, 22 figures — was not retrievable beyond its landing page).
- **How**: SRv6 deterministic source routing pins each probe to an exact path, eliminating ECMP ambiguity and routing-convergence noise so failures localize to specific links even between training jobs; output feeds path-avoidance policy for MRC.
- **Where applicable**: Requires SRv6 (and works best with MRC) in the backend network — OpenAI production, but the technique is general to any SRv6 fabric and notably valuable for small network teams operating huge clusters.

### Beyond Traditional BGP: Decoupling Route Distribution for Large-Scale AI Clusters at Alibaba

**Links:** [Abstract](https://2026ocpglobal.fnvirtual.app/a/schedule/8868/)

Eddie Ruan (Alibaba), Dennis Cai (Alibaba)

- **Why**: Directly in the PM's BGP/datapath-internals interest: a concrete alternative to Microsoft's Fairwater host-SRv6 approach for AI-cluster routing scale, from a provider that can't mandate host-side changes — a real architectural debate with numbers attached.
- **What**: An architectural decoupling of service route distribution from infrastructure route distribution: regular BGP continues to carry infrastructure routes within spine/leaf, while service routes are redistributed among leaf switches by multiple route reflectors, recursively resolved over the loopback of the anchoring leaf switch (from abstract only).

- **Where applicable**: Alibaba-specific architecture for multi-tenant clouds that cannot impose host changes — broadly applicable to any provider facing the same constraint; BGP-only, no host or silicon requirements.

## Themes

- **Ethernet is eating the AI cluster bottom-up**: with ESUN (operator requirements), SUE-T (memory-transaction transport), and Tahan (a real scale-up switch), open Ethernet now claims scale-up alongside scale-out — the last proprietary interconnect domain (in-rack XPU fabrics) is being standardized in the open, ratified in months with 170+ contributors.
- **RoCEv2's successor wars have begun — in the open**: MetaRoCE, OpenAI/Broadcom's MRC, Google's Falcon, and SUE-T are four open transport specs from hyperscalers in one cycle, all converging on multipath + receiver-driven/host-NIC intelligence and abandoning PFC-tuned losslessness as the model. Expect consolidation fights in 2026–27.
- **The WAN is now part of the AI fabric**: scale-across panels, Alibaba's AI DCI/WAN TE, and Meta's SRv6 RBB all treat inter-DC links as a continuation of the training fabric rather than classical DCI — coherent ZR/ZR+ pluggables, deep buffering, and TE for synchronized bursts are the new WAN requirements.
- **Deterministic, microsecond-scale telemetry is the new control loop**: HST/CSIG in SONiC, Clustermapper's SRv6 ground-truth probing, and clock-aligned evidence models share one thesis — statistical inference is being replaced by deterministic measurement as the basis for diagnosis and congestion control.
- **SONiC is maturing on three axes at once**: performance (6–12x route programming via ZMQ/tcmalloc, upstreamed), portability (software-assisted CSIG on older ASICs), and trustworthiness (FIPS, attestation, eBPF live patching) — it is consolidating its position as the open NOS for AI-era fabrics.
- **The physical layer is being decided empirically**: LPO fleet-hours, AEC multi-vendor production data, CPO power deltas, and Oracle's 800G→1.6T field experience mean optics and cabling choices are now driven by measured failure rates and power, not vendor spec sheets — and power oscillation constraints from utilities are becoming a cluster-architecture input.
