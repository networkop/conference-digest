### OAuthHub: Mitigating OAuth Data Overaccess through a Local Data Hub

Authors: Qiyu Li (University of California, San Diego), Yuhe Tian (University of California, San Diego), Haojian Jin (University of California, San Diego)

- **Why**: OAuth over-granting is a chronic production problem — relying parties request broad scopes, users press Allow, and tokens hoover up data forever. A mitigation paper from Jin's group (which works on usable system security at UCSD) addresses the day-to-day OAuth design question every platform PM faces.
- **What**: Apparently a client-side "local data hub" that sits between OAuth flows and relying parties, mediating and limiting the data actually released so that granted scope doesn't equal unlimited access *(title/author line only — no abstract in program)*.
- **How**: Inference from the title: an on-device broker that intercepts token/data handoffs, enforces least-privilege per request, and possibly surfaces granular revocation to the user.
- **Where applicable**: Consumer OAuth/OIDC ecosystems on personal devices (browser or mobile). Browser- or OS-integration dependent; not a server-side-only fix.

- **Where applicable**: VC/DID wallet ecosystems, enterprise credentials, and any selective-disclosure identity scheme; general technique, not tied to one issuer.

### Personalizing Agent Privacy Decisions via Logical Entailment

**Links:** [Recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=azHZ2O1vAIY)

Authors: James Flemings (University of Southern California), Ren Yi (Google), Octavian Suciu (Google), Kassem Fawaz (University of Wisconsin--Madison), Murali Annavaram (University of Southern California), Marco Gruteser (Google)

- **Why**: This is the agentic-identity paper of the program. As AI agents act on users' behalf, the question shifts from authentication ("is this agent who it says it is") to authorization policy ("what may it disclose, to whom, in this context"). A Google co-authorship suggests the problem is real at production scale.
- **What**: Apparently a formal-logic approach to encoding user privacy preferences and checking whether a proposed agent action follows from them — turning agent decisions into a personalized policy-entailment problem *(title/author line only — no abstract in program)*.
- **How**: Inference: user preferences expressed as logical rules; an agent's intended action (or data disclosure) is evaluated by entailment against those rules, with personalization meaning the rule set is learned/derived per user.
- **Where applicable**: Assistant/agent platforms and any system where delegated AI acts for a user — the natural companion to SPIFFE-style workload identity: proving *who* the agent is only matters once you can also decide *what it may do*.

### Ephemeral Network-Layer Fingerprinting Defenses

- **Why**: Passive network-layer fingerprinting (TCP/IP stack and header-level characteristics, p0f-style) is how censors and adversaries identify proxies, bridges, and tunnel traffic without decrypting anything. - **Where applicable**: Circumvention/proxy infrastructure (Tor bridges, VPN endpoints) and any deployment that must survive passive, low-cost classification by a network observer. General technique at the OS/network-stack level.

## Themes

- **Circumvention has become a full systems discipline.** QUIC connection migration, WebRTC media encapsulation, serverless-egress proxies, bridge-reporting pipelines, onion-routing fragmentation, and mixnet latency studies together form an engineering stack — protocol features, cover traffic, infrastructure economics, and user reporting — rather than a set of isolated protocol tricks.
- **Fingerprinting is the arms race of the cycle.** Network-layer stacks, IPv6 addresses, browsers, video content, voice assistants, and Android devices all get dedicated fingerprinting papers — and nearly each attack has a paired client-side defense (ephemeral defenses, Dodge, PriVA-C). Any deployment that assumes "encrypted means unidentifiable" is being disproven layer by layer.
- **Identity infrastructure is absorbing privacy constraints.** Digital-ID risk frameworks, OAuth scope overreach, revocable-but-unlinkable verifiable credentials, and Privacy Pass property analysis show the identity layer being redesigned around correlation resistance — the same forces reshaping passkeys, PATs, and federation.
- **Agentic AI has arrived as a governance problem.** Agent decision logic via logical entailment, automated compliance auditing of AI agents, web-agent security/privacy evals, and GPT-integration dataflow tracing mark the emergence of a policy/authorization surface for AI agents — the natural extension of workload identity into *what the workload may do*.
- **Encrypted computation is getting a cost model.** FHE-for-AI SoKs, query-efficient PIR, HE-accelerated training, and private transformer inference (SPRINT, from SAP) show the field moving from "possible in principle" to dollars-per-inference accounting.
- **Regulation is driving measurement science.** DSA ad-transparency audits, TCF/GDPR compliance analysis, CCPA opt-out usability, and policy-label accuracy studies make legal regimes a first-class experimental target — a trend that increasingly touches anyone operating consumer-facing networked services.
